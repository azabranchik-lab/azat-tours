Azat means “free.”

It's my name — and it's how I want you to feel the day you land. I'm Azat, and I've guided travellers across Kyrgyzstan since 2019. Let me show you my country the way I know it.

Meet the founder

Why I started Azat Tours

I grew up between Issyk-Kul and the Tian Shan, on summers in my grandmother's yurt — woodsmoke, horses, kymyz poured for anyone who passed. Where I'm from, a stranger at the door isn't an interruption. It's a gift.

I started guiding in 2019 because I kept meeting people who'd seen Kyrgyzstan from a bus window but never really met it. I wanted to give them the other version — no fixed script, no tourist bubble. Go at your own pace, sleep under the stars, change the plan when the weather turns perfect. That's what azat means to me: free.

I don't do this alone. I work with guides from Karakol, Naryn and Bishkek who grew up here too — people who know which pass turns dangerous when the clouds come in early, because we've been turned back by it ourselves.

"I don't sell tours. I open the door to my country and say — you're free here. Come in."

Azat — founder & guide, on the trail to Song-Kul

Why we do this

Our mission

To help you feel free in Kyrgyzstan — and completely at home. Free to wander at your own pace, to sit at a herder's table, to rewrite the plan when the mountains call. And to leave the country I love better than we found it: fair pay for the families who host you, clean jailoo, valleys our kids will inherit — not just photos of them.

What we stand for

Travel that gives back

Community first

Safety, no compromise

First-aid-trained guides, vetted vehicles, satellite comms in remote areas and a 24/7 team in Bishkek.
