Entry & visas

Do I need a visa?

Good news: Kyrgyzstan is one of the easiest countries in the region to enter. Citizens of 60+ countries can visit visa-free for up to 60 days, including the EU, UK, USA, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Japan and South Korea.

  • Visa-free 60 days for most Western & East-Asian passports
  • Simple e-visa online for other nationalities (a few days to process)
  • Passport valid 6+ months, with a couple of blank pages
  • No vaccinations required for entry

How to get there

Getting to Kyrgyzstan

Most travellers fly into Manas International Airport (FRU) in Bishkek, the capital. There's no single long-haul hub, so you'll usually connect once.

  • Common connections: Istanbul, Dubai, Almaty, Moscow
  • Osh (OSS) in the south is handy for the Pamir & Fergana valley
  • Overland borders open with Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan & China
  • We meet you at the airport, transfers are included in our tours
Tip

Almaty (Kazakhstan) often has cheaper long-haul flights and is a 3-4 hour drive to the border, ask us if it's worth it for your route.

Peace of mind

Is Kyrgyzstan safe?

Yes, Kyrgyzstan is one of Central Asia's safest and friendliest countries, and a favourite with solo and women travellers. Crime against tourists is rare, and hospitality runs deep in nomadic culture.

  • Very low rates of crime against travellers
  • Popular and comfortable for solo & women travellers
  • Main risks are weather & altitude, managed on guided trips
  • Shepherd dogs on trails: our guides know how to handle them

Budget

Money & costs

Kyrgyzstan is refreshingly affordable for an adventure destination. The currency is the Kyrgyz som (KGS).

  • Carry cash for the mountains, ATMs are only in cities & towns
  • Cards work in Bishkek hotels & supermarkets, rarely in villages
  • USD & EUR are easy to exchange in Bishkek
  • Tipping guides & drivers is appreciated but optional

Be ready

What to pack

Mountain weather changes fast, you can have sun, wind and snow in one day. Layers are everything.

Layers

Base layers, a warm mid-layer (fleece/down) and a waterproof shell. Evenings get cold even in summer.

Footwear

Broken-in waterproof hiking boots for treks; comfy shoes for road trips and towns.

Sleep & sun

Sleeping-bag liner for yurts (we provide bags), sunglasses, high-SPF sunscreen, a hat and lip balm.

Power

Power bank, electricity is limited in yurt camps. EU-style sockets (type C/F), 220V.

Personal kit

Any personal medication, basic first aid, blister plasters and wet wipes for off-grid days.

Bags

A daypack plus a soft duffel, easier than hard cases for transfers and pack horses.

Stay well

Health & altitude

No vaccinations are required to enter, and standard travel jabs are enough for most. The thing to respect is altitude, many highlights sit at 3,000 m+.

  • We build in gradual acclimatisation on higher routes
  • Drink plenty of water; ascend slowly; tell your guide how you feel
  • Drink bottled or treated water off-grid
  • Travel insurance with mountain cover is required on our tours

Staying connected

Internet & SIM cards

Cities and towns have good 4G; deep in the mountains you'll often be happily offline, which is part of the magic.

  • Cheap local SIMs (O!, Beeline, MegaCom) at the airport & in Bishkek
  • Solid coverage around Issyk-Köl and main roads
  • Little or no signal at remote camps & high passes
  • Download offline maps (Maps.me) before you head into the hills
