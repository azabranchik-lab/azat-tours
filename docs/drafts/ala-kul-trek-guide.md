## Текст статьи (английский, в голосе сайта)

**Title:** The Ala-Kul trek: route, difficulty, season and what to expect
**Description:** A local guide's walk-through of the Ala-Kul trek from Karakol to Altyn-Arashan: the route day by day, how hard it is, when to go, what it costs and what to pack.

Ask any traveller what they remember most about Kyrgyzstan and there's a good chance they'll say one word: Ala-Kul. The lake sits at [check: 3,560 m] in the mountains above Karakol and turns a turquoise that looks edited. Getting there takes three or four days on foot and one hard pass. Here is how the trek works, from someone who walks it every summer.

## The trek at a glance

- Route: Karakol valley, Ala-Kul lake, Ala-Kul pass, Altyn-Arashan, Ak-Suu
- Length: about [check: 55 km] over 3 to 4 days
- Highest point: Ala-Kul pass, [check: 3,900 m]
- Season: [check: late June to late September], best from July to early September
- Difficulty: moderate to hard, no technical climbing

## Where is Ala-Kul?

Ala-Kul is in the Terskey Ala-Too range, on the eastern side of Issyk-Kul, a short drive from the town of Karakol. The classic route starts at the gate of Karakol national park, follows the river up through spruce forest, climbs to the lake, crosses the pass above it and drops into the Altyn-Arashan valley with its hot springs. Karakol is about seven hours by road from Bishkek, so most people give the trek four or five days in total.

[img:img/blog/body/ala-kol-pass.jpg|The pass day rewards every step with a panorama over the Tian Shan.]

## The route, day by day

### Day 1: Karakol valley to the first camp

About [check: 15 km and 4 to 5 hours], gaining roughly 600 m. It is a gentle start along the river on a wide track, through forest and open meadow. A taxi to the first bridge cuts off the first few kilometres if you want a shorter day. You sleep near the river, in a tent or a yurt camp.

### Day 2: Up to Ala-Kul lake

The shortest day on the map and the hardest on the legs: about [check: 9.5 km, 5 to 6 hours and more than 1,000 m of climbing]. Past the Sirota camp the path goes almost straight up over roots, rock and gravel. Then the ground levels, and the lake is suddenly below you. Camp is beside the water at around [check: 3,560 m], and it is cold and often windy at night.

### Day 3: Over the pass to Altyn-Arashan

This is the big one, about [check: 15 km and 7 to 8 hours]. You walk along the lake, climb to the pass at [check: 3,900 m], and get the view everyone comes for. The far side is a steep slope of loose scree, and it is the part people find hardest. Poles help, and so does patience. Below it the valley opens out and the day ends in a hot pool at Altyn-Arashan.

### Day 4: Altyn-Arashan to Ak-Suu

About [check: 15 km and 4 hours] down the valley on a rough track, mostly downhill. Some groups take a 4x4 from Altyn-Arashan instead and save their knees.

## How hard is it?

It is a proper trek. You do not need climbing skills, but you do need to be comfortable walking six to eight hours with a pack, and you will sleep above 3,500 m. The height is what catches people out: headaches and poor sleep are common on the second night. Two things make the biggest difference, a night or two in Karakol before you start, and a slow, steady pace on the way up.

[tip:Local tip|Spend a night in Karakol and do a short acclimatisation walk before the pass day. It makes a huge difference to how you feel up high.]

## When to go

The trail is walkable from [check: late June to late September]. July and August are the warmest and busiest months. Early September is quieter, with clear cold mornings. In June there can still be snow on the pass, and after September the yurt camps close and the nights get properly cold. [check: The lake is frozen from about October to May.] Even in midsummer the weather turns fast up there: sun, rain and snow in one afternoon is normal.

> The lake doesn't look real the first time you see it. People go quiet. Then they start taking a hundred photos.

## Fees and permits

You do not need a special trekking permit. You pay an entrance fee at the gate of Karakol national park, [check: about 250 to 400 som per person, plus about 100 som per tent], in cash. Keep the receipt until you are out of the park.

## Where you sleep

There are three options, and you can mix them. Wild camping is allowed along the route if you carry your own tent, mat and stove. In season there are yurt camps at [check: the first camp, at Sirota and below the pass], with a bed and meals. In Altyn-Arashan there are simple guesthouses next to the hot springs. None of the camps have electricity, so bring a power bank.

## Water, food and signal

Streams are frequent all the way, but animals graze above most of them, so filter or boil the water. Carry your own food for the trail unless you have booked meals at the camps. [check: There is no phone signal for most of the route], so download an offline map before you leave Karakol.

## What to pack

Pack for four seasons. Broken-in hiking boots, trekking poles, a warm layer and a down jacket, a waterproof shell, thermals for the night at the lake, a hat and gloves, sunglasses and sunscreen, a headlamp, a water filter and a power bank. Add swimwear for the hot springs. If you camp on your own you also need a tent, a sleeping bag rated for nights around freezing, a mat and a stove.

## Can I do it independently?

You can, and plenty of people do. The path is clear in good weather. What a guide adds is judgement: whether today is a good day for the pass, where the river is safe to cross after rain, and what to do when someone feels the altitude. [check: With us, packhorses carry the bags and camp is set up when you arrive], so you walk with a day pack.

- **How long is the Ala-Kul trek?** About [check: 55 km], usually walked in 3 or 4 days from Karakol to Ak-Suu.
- **How high is the Ala-Kul pass?** About [check: 3,900 m]. The lake below it is at about [check: 3,560 m].
- **When is the best time to do the Ala-Kul trek?** From July to early September. The trail is usually open from [check: late June to late September].
